# Magnetometry Survey of the Lower Town

## Scope and record history

Field measurements were tied to the expedition grid and checked against independent control. Interpretation
distinguishes directly observed anomalies or geological units from archaeological hypotheses that require
excavation for confirmation. Survey coverage was limited by modern tracks, spoil heaps, and steep slopes.
Areas of poor data quality are mapped explicitly so that gaps are not mistaken for archaeologically empty
zones.

## Recording and analytical procedure

Anomalies are described by geometry, amplitude, depth estimate, and relationship to the site grid. Functional
labels such as kiln, wall, or grave remain interpretive until tested by excavation. Geological observations
were compared with samples from known archaeological materials. Source attribution is expressed as closest
match rather than certainty when multiple outcrops share similar petrography.

During archive preparation, drawings, photographs, sample logs, and object registers were reconciled against
the written narrative. Not every recorded observation contributes to the interpretation: colour changes at
baulk edges, isolated body sherds, displaced stones, minor root disturbance, and uncertain joins are retained
because they document the actual evidentiary texture of excavation. Coordinates and measurements were checked
for internal consistency, but values were not normalized merely to make neighbouring records agree. Where the
team used a shorthand description in the field, the expanded wording appears in the discussion while the
original identifier is preserved. This makes the archive suitable for later reanalysis without requiring the
reader to assume that the first interpretation was final.

## Detailed observations

Topographic and geochemical data were normalized within each campaign before cross-season comparison. This
reduces instrument drift but does not remove the effects of soil moisture or later disturbance. The archive
entry retains minor measurements, negative observations, and rejected joins because these often matter when a
later specialist revisits the material.

## Interpretive discussion

The present interpretation gives greatest weight to associations that can be checked independently, while
preserving uncertainty where the record does not support finer resolution.

## Supplementary observations and limitations

Interpretive polygons were drawn only after the raw data had been inspected in multiple renderings. Linear
anomalies aligned with modern agricultural features or expedition infrastructure were separated from
archaeological candidates where possible. The archive preserves both processed images and minimally processed
data so that later researchers can test whether filtering choices created or suppressed particular patterns.

## Register extracts and secondary observations

Archive control for TA-SUR-01 included comparison of 30 photographic frames, 8 registered material or sample
entries, and 3 drawing or measurement checks. The reconciliation produced several minor observations that were
retained even though they did not alter the main interpretation: a modern track creates a linear disturbance
that was masked before archaeological interpretation; data quality declines near the steep edge of the mound
where instrument contact or sensor height was less consistent; and one anomaly changes character after
filtering and is therefore left functionally unclassified. These details are deliberately kept in the research
record because later work may give them significance, but none is promoted beyond the strength of the original
observation.
