# Thermoluminescence Pilot Study of Kiln Furniture

## Scope and record history

The laboratory report distinguishes measured values, quality-control observations, calibration or modelling
choices, and archaeological interpretation. Sample integrity and the relationship between the dated material
and the target event are considered explicitly. Samples were pretreated according to material type, and
quality-control indicators were reviewed before calibration. The report flags samples with possible old-wood,
reservoir, or residual-material problems rather than excluding them silently.

## Recording and analytical procedure

Calibrated ranges are rounded consistently and probability levels are stated where they matter. Modelled
boundaries are not interchangeable with individual sample ranges, so both are reported when Bayesian analysis
is used. The archaeological target event may differ from the biological age of the sample. Short-lived seeds
are preferred for floor destruction or storage events, whereas architectural timbers can predate construction
if reused.

During archive preparation, drawings, photographs, sample logs, and object registers were reconciled against
the written narrative. Not every recorded observation contributes to the interpretation: colour changes at
baulk edges, isolated body sherds, displaced stones, minor root disturbance, and uncertain joins are retained
because they document the actual evidentiary texture of excavation. Coordinates and measurements were checked
for internal consistency, but values were not normalized merely to make neighbouring records agree. Where the
team used a shorthand description in the field, the expanded wording appears in the discussion while the
original identifier is preserved. This makes the archive suitable for later reanalysis without requiring the
reader to assume that the first interpretation was final.

## Detailed observations

Laboratory identifiers and expedition sample numbers are both retained because they serve different tracking
purposes. Tables therefore include both even when only one is normally cited in field documentation. The
archive entry retains minor measurements, negative observations, and rejected joins because these often matter
when a later specialist revisits the material.

## Supplementary observations and limitations

Replicate measurements and background controls were reviewed before archaeological interpretation. Where a
sample was very small, the report notes the resulting uncertainty rather than presenting the calibrated range
with false precision. Calibration curves can create multiple probability peaks, and the preferred summary does
not erase those features. When several determinations are combined, the assumptions required for combination
are stated explicitly.

## Register extracts and secondary observations

Archive control for TA-DAT-07 included comparison of 14 photographic frames, 19 registered material or sample
entries, and 4 drawing or measurement checks. The reconciliation produced several minor observations that were
retained even though they did not alter the main interpretation: sample mass was low enough to widen
uncertainty but not to invalidate the determination; the archaeological event is discussed separately from the
biological age of the dated material; and the calibration distribution contains more than one probability peak
and is not reduced to a single midpoint. These details are deliberately kept in the research record because
later work may give them significance, but none is promoted beyond the strength of the original observation.
