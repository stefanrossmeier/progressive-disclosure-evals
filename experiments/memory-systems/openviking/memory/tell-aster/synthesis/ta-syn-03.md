# Historical Note on the House of Mekar

## Scope and record history

This document integrates several strands of expedition work without replacing the underlying specialist
records. Where interpretations differ between seasons, the reasoning and evidence for the newer view are
stated rather than silently harmonized. Earlier interpretations are quoted where they shaped later work, but
the synthesis distinguishes them from the current view. Revisions are supported by explicit stratigraphic,
specialist, or archival observations.

## Recording and analytical procedure

Chronological discussion separates relative phasing from absolute dates. Ceramic horizons, architectural
episodes, and laboratory ranges overlap imperfectly, and the synthesis avoids forcing them into a single
precision they do not possess. Cross-references are provided to specialist reports so that individual claims
can be checked against primary records. The synthesis does not reproduce complete catalogues, laboratory
tables, or context sheets.

During archive preparation, drawings, photographs, sample logs, and object registers were reconciled against
the written narrative. Not every recorded observation contributes to the interpretation: colour changes at
baulk edges, isolated body sherds, displaced stones, minor root disturbance, and uncertain joins are retained
because they document the actual evidentiary texture of excavation. Coordinates and measurements were checked
for internal consistency, but values were not normalized merely to make neighbouring records agree. Where the
team used a shorthand description in the field, the expanded wording appears in the discussion while the
original identifier is preserved. This makes the archive suitable for later reanalysis without requiring the
reader to assume that the first interpretation was final.

## Detailed observations

Open questions are retained where evidence remains incomplete. Competing explanations are narrowed only when a
new observation actually discriminates between them, rather than because one interpretation is more
convenient. The archive entry retains minor measurements, negative observations, and rejected joins because
these often matter when a later specialist revisits the material.

## Supplementary observations and limitations

The integrated argument was checked against negative evidence as well as positive matches. A proposed
correlation is weaker when an expected wall continuation, ceramic group, botanical signature, or archive
reference is absent from a well-observed area. Such absences are not treated as proof by themselves, but they
help rank competing explanations. The synthesis also records which links remain dependent on a single
excavation observation and which are supported independently by specialist work.

Terminology changed over the long history of the expedition. Older notebooks sometimes use room names, locus
numbers, or informal phase labels that were later replaced by context-based recording. The synthesis gives the
modern identifier when a secure reconciliation exists but retains the older wording in quotations and archival
references. This is important for provenance research because two labels can refer to the same deposit without
implying that the underlying observations were duplicated.

The final narrative deliberately leaves some chronological boundaries broad. Archaeological phases are
interpretive groupings, ceramic horizons are material-culture constructs, and laboratory dates estimate
different target events. Their boundaries need not coincide exactly. Where a narrower date is proposed, the
text identifies the chain of evidence supporting it and avoids treating a convenient midpoint as if it were
directly measured.

Because this archive spans many seasons, the synthesis also checks whether apparent change reflects research
intensity. A phase with more samples, drawings, or specialist attention can look archaeologically richer even
when preservation is comparable. The text therefore notes uneven excavation area, sampling strategy, and
publication history before treating frequency differences as cultural change. This is particularly relevant
when comparing early trenches, which were recorded more selectively, with later campaigns that used systematic
flotation, total-station plotting, and context-based registers.

## Additional analytical caution

The synthesis treats cross-document agreement as evidence only when the records are genuinely independent. A
laboratory date repeated in a season summary is not counted as a second dating observation, and a specialist
identification copied into correspondence is not treated as independent confirmation. This distinction is
important for a retrieval benchmark because the archive contains many references without turning every
repeated statement into a new source of ground truth.

## Appendix-style record notes

Measurements were checked during archive preparation, obvious transcription errors were corrected against the
original sheets, and unresolved discrepancies remain explicitly noted. The document is intended to function as
a realistic research record rather than a compressed answer sheet.
