# Museum Accession Register: 2013 Finds

## Scope and record history

The record documents condition before treatment, materials used, treatment sequence, photography, and storage
recommendations. Interventions were kept reversible where possible and were limited to what was needed for
stabilization and study. Condition was documented photographically before intervention. Soluble salts, active
corrosion, powdering surfaces, and old adhesive residues were assessed because they can alter both appearance
and analytical results.

## Recording and analytical procedure

Mechanical cleaning was undertaken under magnification where possible. Solvents and consolidants were spot-
tested first, and all treatment concentrations are recorded so that later conservators can understand what was
introduced. After treatment, objects were rehoused in inert materials with humidity targets appropriate to
their composition. No reconstruction was attempted where joins were uncertain or would obscure original
surfaces.

During archive preparation, drawings, photographs, sample logs, and object registers were reconciled against
the written narrative. Not every recorded observation contributes to the interpretation: colour changes at
baulk edges, isolated body sherds, displaced stones, minor root disturbance, and uncertain joins are retained
because they document the actual evidentiary texture of excavation. Coordinates and measurements were checked
for internal consistency, but values were not normalized merely to make neighbouring records agree. Where the
team used a shorthand description in the field, the expanded wording appears in the discussion while the
original identifier is preserved. This makes the archive suitable for later reanalysis without requiring the
reader to assume that the first interpretation was final.

## Detailed observations

The conservation record also serves provenance control: object number, context, season, photograph numbers,
and museum accession are checked against packing lists so that treatment does not separate an object from its
excavation history. The archive entry retains minor measurements, negative observations, and rejected joins
because these often matter when a later specialist revisits the material.

## Interpretive discussion

The present interpretation gives greatest weight to associations that can be checked independently, while
preserving uncertainty where the record does not support finer resolution.

## Supplementary observations and limitations

Analytical sampling was minimized and, where undertaken, was recorded as part of the treatment history.
Detached corrosion, textile impressions, pigment particles, and soil accretions were retained when they might
preserve information relevant to later study. Storage recommendations distinguish short-term handling needs
from long-term environmental targets, since a treatment that stabilizes an object during study does not remove
the need for appropriate museum conditions.

## Register extracts and secondary observations

Archive control for TA-CON-03 included comparison of 32 photographic frames, 7 registered material or sample
entries, and 6 drawing or measurement checks. The reconciliation produced several minor observations that were
retained even though they did not alter the main interpretation: detached accretion was retained in a labelled
micro-sample rather than discarded during cleaning; a small test area showed that stronger solvent action
would alter the original surface and was therefore rejected; and pre-treatment and post-treatment photography
used the same scale and orientation where possible. These details are deliberately kept in the research record
because later work may give them significance, but none is promoted beyond the strength of the original
observation.
