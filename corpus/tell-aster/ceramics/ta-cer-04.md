# Kiln Products from Trench T7

## Scope and record history

The assemblage was studied by ware, fabric, surface treatment, form, and degree of preservation. Diagnostic
sherds were counted separately from body sherds, while joins and whole-vessel equivalents were tracked where
the deposits permitted. Fabric groups were established from fresh breaks with a hand lens and were cross-
checked against the expedition reference collection. Surface colour alone was not treated as a reliable
production marker because firing conditions varied considerably.

## Recording and analytical procedure

Rim, base, handle, and body forms were counted separately. Chronological assignments depend on combinations of
diagnostics rather than a single sherd, especially in contexts where residual material is common. Imported
wares were identified through fabric, slip, and form together. Petrographic or chemical work is cited where
available, while visually distinctive pieces without laboratory confirmation remain provisionally classified.

During archive preparation, drawings, photographs, sample logs, and object registers were reconciled against
the written narrative. Not every recorded observation contributes to the interpretation: colour changes at
baulk edges, isolated body sherds, displaced stones, minor root disturbance, and uncertain joins are retained
because they document the actual evidentiary texture of excavation. Coordinates and measurements were checked
for internal consistency, but values were not normalized merely to make neighbouring records agree. Where the
team used a shorthand description in the field, the expanded wording appears in the discussion while the
original identifier is preserved. This makes the archive suitable for later reanalysis without requiring the
reader to assume that the first interpretation was final.

## Detailed observations

Several contexts contain mixed or intrusive material from later cuts. The discussion therefore distinguishes
the date of an assemblage's main deposition from the full range of sherd dates represented within it. The
archive entry retains minor measurements, negative observations, and rejected joins because these often matter
when a later specialist revisits the material.

## Supplementary observations and limitations

Quantification was repeated after cross-mending because several apparently separate rims proved to belong to
single vessels. The report keeps both raw diagnostic counts and adjusted vessel estimates so that later
researchers can reproduce frequency calculations. Abrasion, soot, lime accretion, and secondary burning were
recorded because these traces can distinguish cooking use, storage, disposal, and post-depositional exposure.
Sherds from floor make-up are treated especially cautiously: they may date the preparation material rather
than the use of the floor itself. Conversely, vessels crushed in place on a floor can provide a much tighter
association when joins and spatial distribution support primary deposition.

The local sequence is built from recurring combinations rather than rigid type fossils. A vessel form can
persist across more than one horizon, and imported wares may arrive late or remain in circulation. For this
reason, a context is not assigned to a horizon merely because one diagnostic sherd is present. Assemblage
size, residuality, intrusive cuts, and the proportion of later forms are weighed together. The catalogue
includes minority fabrics and unusual shapes even when they do not influence the final date, because those
outliers are often the material most likely to matter in future provenance or exchange studies.

Catalogue tables retain fragments that were excluded from chronological counts, such as heavily abraded rims,
pieces with uncertain joins, and sherds from disturbed interfaces. This prevents the analytical sample from
becoming indistinguishable from the complete recovered assemblage. Drawings were selected to represent both
common and unusual forms, and scale was checked against the original profile sheets during archive
preparation. Where a fabric designation changed after petrographic comparison, the earlier field code is
preserved in the concordance so that bag labels and old notes remain traceable.

## Additional analytical caution

The study also tracks uncertainty in classification. Sherds that fall near a fabric boundary remain in an
indeterminate group instead of being forced into the nearest named fabric, and unusual profiles are drawn even
when only one example is known. This preserves the distinction between a genuinely rare form and an analytical
category created by incomplete preservation. Such records provide realistic distractors without changing the
principal chronological conclusions.
