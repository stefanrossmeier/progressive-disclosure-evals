# Seed and Fruit Remains from the Granary Block

## Scope and record history

Samples were processed using the expedition's standard flotation or specialist laboratory protocols. Counts,
ubiquity, preservation, and contamination risks are considered before interpretation, with particular
attention to whether deposits are primary or redeposited. Sample volume and recovery method strongly affect
counts. The report therefore uses both absolute counts and relative abundance, while noting contexts in which
preservation or small sample size makes comparison unreliable.

## Recording and analytical procedure

Charred plant material can enter deposits through fuel, food preparation, accidental burning, or redeposition.
Chaff-rich samples are interpreted differently from clean grain concentrations, and weed ecology is used as
supporting rather than decisive evidence. Faunal identification follows conservative taxonomic categories
where sheep and goat cannot be separated securely. Butchery marks, burning, fragmentation, and age profiles
are recorded because they help distinguish food waste from craft or ritual deposition.

During archive preparation, drawings, photographs, sample logs, and object registers were reconciled against
the written narrative. Not every recorded observation contributes to the interpretation: colour changes at
baulk edges, isolated body sherds, displaced stones, minor root disturbance, and uncertain joins are retained
because they document the actual evidentiary texture of excavation. Coordinates and measurements were checked
for internal consistency, but values were not normalized merely to make neighbouring records agree. Where the
team used a shorthand description in the field, the expanded wording appears in the discussion while the
original identifier is preserved. This makes the archive suitable for later reanalysis without requiring the
reader to assume that the first interpretation was final.

## Detailed observations

Environmental evidence is strongest when it converges with architecture and stratigraphy. The report therefore
notes potentially relevant installations and rooms but leaves broader functional synthesis to documents that
integrate those records. The archive entry retains minor measurements, negative observations, and rejected
joins because these often matter when a later specialist revisits the material.

## Interpretive discussion

The present interpretation gives greatest weight to associations that can be checked independently, while
preserving uncertainty where the record does not support finer resolution.

## Supplementary observations and limitations

Taphonomy is central to interpretation. Dense charred grain can result from a burnt store, but it can also
derive from repeated hearth cleaning; animal bone concentrations may reflect meals, carcass processing, craft
use, or redeposited refuse. Fragmentation, charring intensity, articulation, weed spectra, and the ratio of
crop by-products to clean grain are therefore reported together. Samples from fills are compared with sealed
floor deposits before functional claims are made. Where modern roots or insect activity affected a sample, the
affected taxa are flagged rather than automatically discarded.

Spatial comparisons use standardized counts only after accounting for processed volume. A small sample with a
high percentage can be less informative than a large sample with moderate density, so both concentration and
ubiquity are shown in the underlying tables. Rare taxa are retained because they may document imported food,
local microhabitats, or accidental inclusions, but single specimens are not used to infer economic importance.
Environmental conclusions become stronger when repeated across independent contexts or when they match
architectural installations and stratigraphic evidence.

Reference collections and modern comparative material were consulted for difficult identifications, but the
report records the level at which each specimen can actually be distinguished. Broad categories are preferred
to overconfident species calls when diagnostic structures are missing. Counts used in interpretation can
therefore differ from the number of fragments examined. This conservative approach is especially important for
fragmented charcoal, caprine bone, and small seeds, where preservation can remove the characters needed for
finer identification.

## Additional analytical caution

The environmental archive includes many low-count observations that do not drive interpretation. Small numbers
of weed seeds, fish vertebrae, shell fragments, and charcoal taxa are retained because future comparative work
may make them meaningful. At present, stronger conclusions rely on repeated patterning, concentration,
association, and taphonomic consistency rather than on isolated specimens.

## Final record-quality note

Subsamples used for specialist identification were linked back to their parent flotation or sediment samples
so that density calculations remain reproducible. The report distinguishes material counted in the full sample
from material examined only in a selected fraction. This prevents a detailed taxonomic observation from being
mistaken for a quantitative abundance measure and makes it possible to compare contexts processed under
slightly different laboratory workflows.
