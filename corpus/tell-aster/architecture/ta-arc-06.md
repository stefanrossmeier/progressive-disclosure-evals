# Temple Precinct Masonry Catalogue

## Scope and record history

The survey combines total-station control, scaled hand measurement, stone-by-stone drawing, and observations
of bonding and abutment. Dimensions refer to preserved faces unless otherwise stated, and reconstructions are
separated from direct measurements. Wall faces were recorded by course, with stone dimensions sampled at
regular intervals. Bonding was examined at corners and junctions, and plaster scars were noted where they
preserved room divisions no longer represented by standing masonry.

## Recording and analytical procedure

Door widths and circulation routes were reconstructed from surviving jambs rather than from gaps alone. Where
later robbing removed thresholds, the plan marks the opening as probable. Rebuilding episodes can often be
recognized through changes in masonry size and foundation depth. Floor elevations show that adjacent rooms
were not necessarily contemporary even when they share a wall line. Threshold steps, blocked openings, and
added benches reveal a sequence of modification that complicates purely geometric interpretation.

During archive preparation, drawings, photographs, sample logs, and object registers were reconciled against
the written narrative. Not every recorded observation contributes to the interpretation: colour changes at
baulk edges, isolated body sherds, displaced stones, minor root disturbance, and uncertain joins are retained
because they document the actual evidentiary texture of excavation. Coordinates and measurements were checked
for internal consistency, but values were not normalized merely to make neighbouring records agree. Where the
team used a shorthand description in the field, the expanded wording appears in the discussion while the
original identifier is preserved. This makes the archive suitable for later reanalysis without requiring the
reader to assume that the first interpretation was final.

## Detailed observations

Capacity estimates for bins and rooms use preserved dimensions and conservative reconstructed heights. They
are intended as comparative values, not exact ancient volumes, because upper wall courses and organic
superstructures are lost. The archive entry retains minor measurements, negative observations, and rejected
joins because these often matter when a later specialist revisits the material.

## Interpretive discussion

The present interpretation gives greatest weight to associations that can be checked independently, while
preserving uncertainty where the record does not support finer resolution.

## Supplementary observations and limitations

Construction materials vary across the surveyed area. Mudbrick dimensions were measured only where complete
arrises survived, because swollen or eroded bricks can exaggerate original size. Stone socles often contain
reused blocks, including pieces with plaster or dressing inconsistent with their present position. Such reuse
complicates attempts to date walls from masonry alone. The survey therefore gives priority to foundations,
bonded junctions, sealed floor relationships, and doorway modifications when reconstructing building history.
In rooms where later benches obscure earlier wall faces, the drawings distinguish visible masonry from
inferred continuation, and the text records how much of each reconstruction is actually supported.

Spatial interpretation was tested against practical movement rather than plan symmetry. Door swings are
unknown, but threshold wear, step placement, and the width of openings suggest which routes carried repeated
traffic. Some rooms that look central on plan are awkward to reach, while small side chambers sit on efficient
routes between courtyards and storage spaces. Light and ventilation are considered only where architectural
evidence supports openings; roof height and upper storeys remain uncertain. The report therefore avoids
assigning functions purely from room size and instead combines access, installations, floor treatment, and
recoveries documented in the excavation and specialist archives.

## Register extracts and secondary observations

Archive control for TA-ARC-06 included comparison of 24 photographic frames, 22 registered material or sample
entries, and 6 drawing or measurement checks. The reconciliation produced several minor observations that were
retained even though they did not alter the main interpretation: several stones show earlier plaster on
concealed faces, indicating reuse without establishing the date of their first setting; threshold wear is
asymmetric and may reflect repeated movement, although the direction of travel cannot be reconstructed; and
one wall face bows slightly where later pressure displaced the upper courses. These details are deliberately
kept in the research record because later work may give them significance, but none is promoted beyond the
strength of the original observation.

## Additional analytical caution

A separate check was made for features that could bias functional interpretation, including robbed thresholds,
later blocking walls, installations cut by subsequent floors, and masonry reused from earlier structures. The
measured plan is therefore not treated as a snapshot of one perfectly preserved building phase. Rooms are
compared through combinations of access, floor elevation, wall bonding, fixed installations, and recoveries
rather than through geometry alone.

## Final record-quality note

A final drawing check compared wall faces, thresholds, and fixed installations against the photographic
register rather than relying on the clean plan alone. Small discrepancies usually reflect erosion or the
choice of measurement point, not separate construction phases. Where an element was partly concealed, the plan
uses a broken reconstruction line and the narrative states the basis for continuation. This keeps visually
tidy architectural drawings from overstating what actually survived and gives later readers enough information
to distinguish measurement from restoration.
